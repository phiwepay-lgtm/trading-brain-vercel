# TradingView Indicator Algo V1 — first research draft

This is a provisional implementation of the material supplied in November 2026, **not** a verified automation of the trading edge. The Pine source has not been compiled or run in TradingView. It does not trade or connect to MT5. A TradingView strategy draft and an MT5 indicator/tester-only EA draft are also provided. A live/demo EA has **not** been implemented. Do not use this for live decisions until the assumptions and historical behavior have been checked against actual examples.

## Interpretation used in this draft

- Only standard M1 and M5 charts are accepted. M5 Heikin Ashi data is requested internally, without looking into future completed candles. The M1 execution chart uses normal OHLC. On M5 only the 0.382 monitor operates; M1 confirmations and positions require M1.
- A provisional M5 impulse starts when the HA close crosses the latest confirmed HA pivot high or low, using the last opposite pivot as the other anchor. Pivot detection uses configurable left/right bars, default 2/2. The extreme can extend until the first later M5 HA bar touches the 0.382 level; at that point both anchors are frozen. If an HA bar touches or extends the trend-side extreme and also crosses the fib, the draft ignores that same-bar fib touch because their order is unknown. This is **not** a definitive implementation of visual clarity, a separately validated BOS for each pivot, or the user's discretionary anchor selection.
- A confirmed 0.382 touch starts the trend-direction setup without requiring a second M5 pivot break. The draft does not separately verify that the level was respected. For FTB this draft requires a subsequent HA candle moving toward the frozen pivot with a higher high (uptrend) or lower low (downtrend), then an opposite-color HA close whose wick remains strictly short of that pivot. A wick touch without a closing break cancels the setup rather than qualifying as FTB. This conservative choice can miss a later continuation. It cannot know intent or whether an arbitrary candle truly represents an attempt. A setup expires after 48 M5 bars by default. Both scenarios require the initial touch. Direction reverses only for FTB.
- M1 CHoCH is approximated by a close crossing the most recent confirmed M1 pivot high only when that high is lower than the preceding confirmed high (long), or crossing the most recent pivot low only when that low is higher than the preceding confirmed low (short). This two-pivot comparison is a mechanical proxy, not guaranteed visual structure. The **very next** M1 candle must be an opposite-color full-body engulf; if not, the pending setup is discarded. Because that definition requires the CHoCH candle to be opposite-colored, it may exclude examples where the user considers a same-direction pair an engulf. The model does not prove the engulf itself formed a new HH/HL or LL/LH. There is no separate sweep gate. Pivot, engulf, signal sequence, expiry and stop selection require user/chart validation. The wait for CHoCH expires after 240 bars by default, and a newer M5 setup can replace a pending one.
- The entry uses the engulf close, with a stop beyond the most recent opposite M1 pivot and a configurable 10-tick buffer. There is one virtual position at a time. Fixed targets are 2, 3, 4, 5 or 7 R and optional breakeven triggers are 2 or 3 R. Fixed targets are separate from the original fib 0.0 / −0.5 targets. A configurable structure-exit proxy is also available: after entry, a newly confirmed higher low (long) or lower high (short) becomes a protected pivot, and a later close beyond that level exits the position. A more protective later confirmed pivot replaces it. No structure exit is available until a qualifying post-entry pivot has been confirmed. This is one mechanical interpretation of following structure, not a validated match to the user’s discretionary exit. No partial TP, costs, spread, broker fills or persistence outside loaded history are modeled.
- No entry-bar exit is modeled. When a later OHLC bar hits both stop and target, stop wins. A breakeven trigger takes effect after its bar closes, so the stop cannot fill at breakeven during that same bar. Stop and target checks take precedence over a structure exit on the same OHLC bar; the structure exit occurs at the bar close. If TP occurs before or on the trigger bar, TP is resolved first. A missing M1 bar censors any open virtual position and clears the pending setup, without an exit result. The line plots are active virtual levels, not a full trade ledger. Live colors and historical rendering do not prove execution.
- M1 pivot dots are back-plotted to their pivot bars but are only available after `right` bars; they are **not** known at the earlier plotted time. M5 dots appear when the completed pivot confirmation becomes available, not at the pivot's original timestamp.

## Monitoring and limitations

Use a standard M5 chart and create a TradingView alert for **Any alert() function call**. Up to nine additional, user-configurable TradingView symbols can be enabled. Alerts fire only after an M5 HA bar closes, when the next M5 period exists, and after the chart bar closes: on M5 this adds up to another five minutes; on M1 up to one minute. Closed market gaps, unavailable feeds, invalid symbols, and charts that do not update can prevent alerts. Default symbols are examples and must be changed to the desired feed. Alert infrastructure is TradingView's; the website is not a scanner and no background execution is guaranteed. The same symbol can be selected twice, causing duplicates. Recreate TradingView alerts after changing script or input settings.

Historical results and relative timing vary with feed, session, chart history, and synthetic HA values. A human must compare the M5 anchors, reaction, event, M1 CHoCH and engulf against representative charts, including failure-without-touch, gaps, entry/exit timing and both directions, before trusting or porting the implementation. The user-provided illustrations contain contradictory wording about sweeps, visual breaks, FTB attempts and target anchors; this draft follows the latest no-separate-sweep instruction and records the choices above rather than claiming they were confirmed.

## TradingView Strategy Tester draft

`TradingView_Algo_V2_Study_Strategy.pine` uses the same provisional signal and virtual-position code and additionally submits simulated TradingView strategy entries and brackets. On a standard M1 chart, entry orders are processed on the signal bar's close using `process_orders_on_close`; actual live execution at that price is not guaranteed. Configure Strategy Properties for sizing, capital, slippage and commission as appropriate. The native broker emulator uses its own intrabar assumptions, so its fills and report can diverge from the displayed stop-first virtual simulation. A native position crossing a missing M1 interval is closed at the next available bar close and is not censored: treat that outcome as unreliable. Virtual eligibility continues to gate new entries, so discrepancies can cause missed or skipped native trades. No MT5/live orders are placed. This strategy has not been compiled or run in TradingView.

## MT5 research package

Extract the `MQL5` folder in `Algo_V1_MT5_Research.zip` into the MT5 data folder (File → Open Data Folder), then compile `Indicators/AlgoV1Research.mq5` and `Experts/AlgoV2TesterResearch.mq5` in MetaEditor. `Include/AlgoV1Core.mqh` is required by both. **These files have not been compiled in MetaEditor or tested in MT5.**

The indicator uses standard M1 price data, calculates its own M5 HA history, shows confirmed-candidate arrows, virtual entry/stop/target lines and M1 pivot dots. M1 dots are back-plotted and only known after pivot confirmation; M5 HA dots appear on the M1 bar where confirmation is processed. It replays a bounded M1 history (5,000 bars by default) when a new M1 bar starts. M5 HA is seeded from the oldest downloaded M5 bar, so results can depend on history and differ from TradingView. There is no MT5 multi-symbol scanner or M5 chart mode in this draft. The first 0.382 touch has the same provisional constraints and latency as the indicator, subject to broker history. Data gaps censor the virtual position, not broker fills. This implementation's tie handling and sampling have not been validated for platform parity.

The EA is **hard-restricted to MT5 Strategy Tester**, M1 only, and simulated order placement defaults off (`EnableTesterOrders=false`). It refuses initialization outside the tester, requires a flat tester account and places at most one account-wide position at a time. When explicitly enabled, native tester orders occur on the first tick after an M1 signal closes, using broker quotes and a stop derived from the virtual signal; TP is a fixed R multiple of the quoted risk. Unlike the visual model's bar-close BE, its native BE is monitored on ticks after entry and can be rejected by broker stop/freeze rules. With `UseStructureExit` enabled, the tester attempts to close its identified position on the first tick after the confirmed structure-break candle; failure or a nonmatching position is not treated as a successful close. Tester fills, spread, slippage, gaps, stop and BE timing can differ from the virtual replay and the Pine broker emulator. It cannot reconstruct BE risk if a position cannot be identified after submission, and disables BE for that position; an EA restart with any open position is refused. Review all tester journal messages and reports. No orders are allowed on live or demo accounts; no EX5 is supplied. Backtest results are not evidence of profitability.

## V2 study additions

Both Pine drafts draw bounded horizontal entry, stop and 2/3/4/5/7R levels for the most recent candidate signals (40 sets by default, maximum 60). They also draw historical M1 CHoCH lines and show the currently active M5 HA anchor prices and 0.382 as historical plot segments on standard M1 or M5. These are provisional anchors, not a separately validated complete fib placement or a proof that the level was tradable. Old drawings are discarded when the configured limit is reached; platform history and object limits also apply.

On standard M1 the table evaluates independent hypothetical 2R, 3R, 4R, 5R and 7R exits from the same candidate entry and stop, while displaying counts for 2R, 3R and 5R. The day and Monday-start week windows are calendar periods in the symbol exchange timezone; they group by signal bar-open time against the last chart bar. They include at most the latest 200 candidates by default (maximum 500), so older entries may be omitted even inside the window. The eligible candidates depend on the primary target preset because that preset controls the one-position signal gate. Each target is simulated independently, not as a split position. Open and gap-censored outcomes are excluded from the reported TP percentage; breakeven and structure-exit outcomes are included in its denominator (TP / (TP + SL + BE + STRUCT)). Structure exits are counted separately and are not classified as wins or losses. No spread, costs or intrabar path is modeled. Stop wins a stop/target tie; BE becomes active after the trigger bar closes; missing M1 bars censor remaining scenarios. These statistics are not the native TradingView tester's results, and are not a verified win rate. Calendar windows, historical results and drawings depend on loaded history, settings and feed.

The MT5 tester EA's optional visual mode draws candidate study levels, confirmed M1/M5 pivot dots, M1 CHoCH lines, and bounded historical M5 anchor/0.382 segments while the tester runs. Its default maximum is 40 level sets, 500 pivot dots, 100 CHoCH lines and 100 five-minute fib segments. M1 pivot dots are back-plotted; M5 dots show confirmation time. Drawings are based on research candidates, not actual tester fills. The tester still opens one optional position at the chosen TP preset; it does **not** split positions among target levels or provide a separate broker equity curve for each level. Re-run separate tests for each preset; even then results depend on the signal gate, execution and tester data. MT5 multi-symbol scanning and live/demo trading remain unavailable.

## Scanner, numeric study export and tester deal CSV

The Pine scanner table reports the latest **completed** M5 HA state for the chart symbol and up to nine enabled symbols. `TOUCH` means that completed bar met this draft's strict 0.382 touch conditions, not a confirmed entry or guarantee of a trade; `WATCH` is an active level without that touch, `NO SETUP`, `STALE`, `NO DATA` and `OFF` are distinct. Inactive, stale and unavailable symbols are never presented as ready. Prices use a generic decimal format and are not guaranteed to match each broker's tick size. Alerts use the same completed-bar logic and timing documented above. The scanner does not keep working independently of a running TradingView alert, and TradingView plan and alert limits apply.

The study scripts expose numeric per-signal direction, entry, stop and 2/3/4/5/7R levels in the Data Window on M1. These are hypothetical levels, not outcome records or live orders. TradingView's chart-data CSV export may include these plotted columns depending on platform/version and plan; verify the actual exported file. A Pine table, label, or Data Window value is not a CSV export button and does not automatically send data to the website. The strategy's native trade list can instead be exported from the Strategy Tester where TradingView supports it. The current website viewer below accepts only the MT5 tester deal format, not TradingView chart CSV.

In MT5 Strategy Tester, `SaveTesterDealsCSV` is off by default. When explicitly enabled, the EA writes a uniquely named `AlgoV2_Tester_Deals_*.csv` in the tester agent's Files sandbox, not necessarily the terminal's ordinary `MQL5/Files` folder. It logs matching tester **deal events**, including entry and exit/partial deals, not full trade records, hypothetical R outcomes or guaranteed complete performance. The columns are `time_broker,symbol,deal_id,position_id,deal_type,entry_type,volume,price,profit,commission,swap,currency,reason`; broker timestamps have no timezone/UTC offset. Per-deal profit, commission and swap are broker-emulator values in the named account currency. A missing callback, history selection or write error can leave incomplete records; inspect the tester journal and reconcile with its native report. File writing is sandboxed, with no network or Google Sheets integration. No live/demo execution or logging is enabled by this EA.

In this Vercel-hosted app, **EA Center → Review MT5 tester CSV** opens `/ea/results`. Its parser accepts only the exact CSV schema above, rejects malformed or duplicate deal IDs, and reads up to 4 MB / 10,000 deal rows entirely in the current browser page. It does not send data to a server, persist it, add it to the JSON journal, infer complete trades or calculate win rates. The displayed sum combines profit, commission and swap only within each currency and may be incomplete; it is not verified broker performance. The first 250 rows are shown, while totals use every parsed row. Refreshing or clearing removes the in-memory data. The website does not pull from a VPS, TradingView or Google Sheets; a user must explicitly select the file. No credentials or external spreadsheet connection are configured.

The M5 chart currently displays the scanner and M5 fib plots only. The M1 signal engine and performance recap do **not** run in the background on M5; use a standard M1 chart for those. Building a true M5-hosted, lower-timeframe historical dashboard remains outstanding and cannot be inferred from the sample code, which calculates its "M1" pivots on the chart timeframe and never updates its TP win counters.

## User-supplied V3 reference — not a validated replacement

`M5_HA_Brain_V3_User_Reference.pine` preserves the user-supplied logic separately (with a valid `//@version=5` directive and explanatory warning comments). It is not a verified chart match or an MT5 EA and has not been compiled in TradingView. No actual XAUUSD September 24 chart, feed, year, timezone, or marked entry was available here for comparison.

The provided code has serious limitations:

- With the default `chochLen=5`, a confirmed pivot is at least five bars old, but both entries require `bar_index - lastPivotBar <= 3`. Those entry conditions cannot pass at the default setting. They also require CHoCH and engulf on the **same** bar, not an engulf immediately after CHoCH.
- The M5 pivot calls use regular M5 high/low rather than Heikin Ashi pivots. The custom HA open is recursively updated on the chart timeframe, not within the M5 request. `bosLen` is unused and the code crosses sparse pivot outputs rather than persistently tracking a validated BOS swing.
- Its 0.382 calculation uses the current close as one endpoint, so it moves with price and is not a fixed pivot-to-pivot fib. Entry conditions do not require a 0.382 touch or respect. FTB is absent, bullish trades do not receive the blue risk/target box, and `beAt` only draws a line; it does not manage breakeven.
- The orange label is recreated on every bar while the pivot is set; drawings are subject to TradingView limits. No multi-symbol scanner, trade outcomes or MT5 V3 implementation are supplied by this reference. Do not rely on it for trading or assert it matches a screenshot without a platform test.

## Structure-following proxy and synthetic testing

The default exit mode includes a fixed TP and structure exit. Set `Exit mode` to `Fixed TP only` in Pine, or `UseStructureExit=false` in MT5, to disable structure exits. Structure is represented by confirmed M1 pivots, with the configured confirmation delay. The qualifying higher-low/lower-high pivot must occur on or after the entry bar. The model exits on a completed candle close below/above the protected pivot; it does not exit simply because no new pivot forms and does not establish that this mechanical rule matches discretionary HH/HL or LL/LH interpretation. On MT5 the native tester closes at the next available tick, not the historical close. The hypothetical recap evaluates structure exits separately for each target scenario; it does not calculate P/L from those exits.

The repository includes synthetic state-machine tests using a C++-adapted copy of the shared MT5 core. These check mirrored structure exits, disabled mode, stop/target tie handling, gap censoring and the immediate-engulf timing. They are not a MetaEditor compiler, TradingView compiler, broker test, historical chart comparison or live validation.
